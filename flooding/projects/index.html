Project 1

Catch wildfire smoke early

Mountain-top cameras watch for the first puff of smoke. Can AI raise the alarm within minutes?

Valley Fire, 5 Sep 2020, Lyons Peak camera · FIgLib, HPWREN (hpwren.ucsd.edu)

Why it matters

Same fire, same camera, a few minutes apart.

Fire crews don't count accuracy.
They count minutes.

So we score a detector by minutes to alarm and false alarms per day, not by a single accuracy number.

Zoomed crops of real FIgLib frames, Valley Fire 2020 · HPWREN (hpwren.ucsd.edu)

Trained on some cameras. Tested on cameras it has never seen.

Project 2

Find the rare animals

Motion-triggered cameras take millions of photos. Can AI spot the animals that matter most?

Mountain lion, Caltech Camera Traps (LILA BC), CDLA-Permissive

The problem

Common animals are easy. The rare ones matter.

Why this project is exciting

Help scientists protect animals they can't watch themselves.

Wolverine on a trail camera, Glacier National Park (NPS), public domain

Project 3

Map floods from space

Radar satellites see through clouds, day and night. Here, red is new flood water along a river in Portugal.

Great over farmland. Nearly blind in towns and forests.

Your own idea to test

Water flows downhill. Can the model use that?

Napier, NZ, 14 Feb 2023 · Pierre Markuse, CC BY 2.0 (modified Copernicus Sentinel data)

Look where the water sits: in the low ground along the rivers.

Give the model one extra free map: how high each spot is above the nearest river.

Does that fix the blind spots in forests and towns?
